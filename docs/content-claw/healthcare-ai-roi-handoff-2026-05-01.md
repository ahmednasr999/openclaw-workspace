## Caption draft

Most healthcare AI ROI dashboards are measuring the wrong thing.

They measure the model.

Accuracy.
Latency.
Adoption.
Number of use cases launched.

Those are useful signals, but they are not ROI.

In healthcare, AI ROI shows up only when a workflow changes in a measurable way.

For example:
- Clinician documentation time reduced
- Referral or authorization turnaround improved
- Denials reduced
- AR days shortened
- Readmissions lowered
- Patient experience improved
- Staff capacity redeployed to higher-value work

The mistake is treating AI as a technology implementation.

It is not.

A serious AI initiative is an operating model change:
1. Pick one workflow with clear ownership
2. Define the baseline before AI
3. Measure clinical, operational, and financial impact together
4. Keep a human accountability point
5. Scale only after the workflow proves value

If the dashboard cannot show what changed for clinicians, patients, operations, or cash flow, it is probably not an ROI dashboard.

It is an activity report.

And activity reports are how organizations convince themselves that pilots are progress.

The better question is not: “Did the AI work?”

The better question is: “Which healthcare workflow improved, by how much, and who owns sustaining the gain?”

What would you measure first in a healthcare AI initiative: clinician time saved, patient outcomes, or financial leakage?
