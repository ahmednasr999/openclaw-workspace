# Interview and Gap-Fill Playbook

## High-yield questioning

Ask only questions that change the value or credibility of a story. Prefer one short cluster at a time.

1. **Trigger:** What problem or decision made this work necessary?
2. **Ownership:** What were you personally accountable for?
3. **Decision:** What call did you make that changed the direction?
4. **Resistance:** What constraint, conflict, or trade-off made it difficult?
5. **Action:** What did you personally do that another leader might not have done?
6. **Proof:** What changed, how was it measured, and over what period?
7. **Attribution:** Which result was yours, shared with the team, or simply company context?
8. **Lesson:** What operating rule did you carry into later roles?
9. **Disclosure:** Is any part confidential, approximate, or unsuitable for public use?

Do not ask Ahmed to restate facts already present in approved files.

## S-R-A-R-R answer shape

Use Situation, Responsibility, Action, Result, Reflection for executive interviews:

- **Situation:** one sentence that establishes scale and tension.
- **Responsibility:** one sentence naming Ahmed's remit.
- **Action:** two to four specific decisions or interventions.
- **Result:** one or two supported outcomes with careful attribution.
- **Reflection:** one leadership principle relevant to the target role.

Keep the answer at 60-90 seconds unless Ahmed asks for a deeper version.

## Attribution language

Use language that makes leadership credible rather than inflated:

- Direct: "I built the PMO, recruited the team, and introduced KPI-driven governance."
- Shared: "During that period, the platform scaled from 30,000 to 7 million daily orders. My contribution was product prioritization and operating discipline across the regional teams."
- Contextual: "The company was entering a rapid-growth period; I was not responsible for the entire commercial result."

## Interview story selection

Rank candidate stories on:

1. relevance to the question
2. strength of Ahmed's direct action
3. evidence quality
4. measurable scope or result
5. difference from stories already used in the interview
6. disclosure safety

Avoid answering every question with the same flagship story. Rotate across healthcare, fintech, e-commerce, multi-country PMO, consulting, and technical delivery where evidence supports it.

## Gap policy

If the result is missing, do not invent one. Use a scope-based close such as:

"The verified evidence shows the governance model covered 300+ concurrent projects across eight countries. I would want Ahmed to confirm delivery-rate or financial outcomes before adding them."

If the action is missing, classify the record as partial and ask what Ahmed personally decided or changed.
