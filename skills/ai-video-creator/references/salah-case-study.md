# Standardized Example: Fictional Football Reveal Case Study

This example restructures the imported Salah × Trabzonspor document into a production pattern. It is a fictional, unofficial concept—not evidence of a transfer, endorsement, club approval, or likeness permission.

## 1. Rights and disclosure gate

Before any public or commercial use, record the status of:

- Mohamed Salah's likeness and name;
- Trabzonspor marks, jersey design, sponsor marks, stadium imagery, and supporter footage;
- music, crowd recordings, photographs, and source references;
- the fictional transfer premise.

Required public-facing disclosure when used as a speculative case study:

> Fictional AI concept. Not an official announcement or endorsement by Mohamed Salah, Trabzonspor, sponsors, or any league.

If the appropriate rights are not available, replace the real player with a fictional athlete and use an original club, jersey, stadium, and marks.

## 2. What the source gets right

- It treats character, jersey, and stadium references as a system.
- It uses a strong reveal ladder: atmosphere, jersey, number, name, back, profile, face, hero.
- It expands the story from the pitch into fans, children, stores, and the city.
- It understands that the biggest reveal should be earned.

The production rewrite separates the original multi-scene prompts into one clip per row and moves exact names, numbers, and title cards into tracked post graphics or approved wardrobe plates.

## 3. Reference bible

| ID | Lock | Production note |
|---|---|---|
| `CHAR-01` | Approved likeness, age appearance, facial structure, hair, proportions | Use only within the confirmed rights scope |
| `WARD-01` | Approved jersey cut, palette, stripe layout, collar, fabric, mark placement | Composite exact crest, sponsor, name, and number when required |
| `ENV-01` | Approved stadium architecture and scale | Do not imply location authenticity from a generic generated stadium |
| `ENV-02` | Reference-grounded Trabzon streets, stores, signage, weather, and matchday behavior | Validate with local evidence or reviewer |
| `STYLE-01` | Premium live-action football campaign with believable contrast and motion | Avoid artificial CGI crowds |
| `AUDIO-01` | Suspense build, crowd swell, reveal impact, emotional release | Use licensed or original audio only |

Replace instructions such as “authentically Turkish facial features” with reference-grounded casting, real locations, plausible behavior, local wardrobe, language, and stakeholder review. National identity is not a face template.

## 4. Reveal sequence

Example 20-second structure:

| Shot | Time | Dur. | One generated action | Reveal job | Acceptance focus |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| `S001` | 0.0 | 2.0 | Slow move toward approved stadium exterior | Establish place and anticipation | Architecture and crowd scale |
| `S002` | 2.0 | 1.5 | Supporter raises scarf in a packed stand | Build energy | Natural behavior; stable hands |
| `S003` | 3.5 | 1.5 | Jersey fabric moves on a supporter | Tease wardrobe | Approved color and stripe layout |
| `S004` | 5.0 | 1.5 | Store employee opens one jersey box | Signal announcement | One action; no generated text |
| `S005` | 6.5 | 1.5 | Camera rises over approved back plate | Reveal number `10` in post/plate | Exact approved typography |
| `S006` | 8.0 | 1.5 | Camera continues over a second approved plate | Reveal `SALAH` in post/plate | Exact spelling; no mutation |
| `S007` | 9.5 | 2.0 | Player stands with back to camera | Connect name to player | Likeness hidden; jersey locked |
| `S008` | 11.5 | 2.0 | Camera arcs from back to side profile | First likeness reveal | `CHAR-01` remains stable |
| `S009` | 13.5 | 2.5 | Player steps from tunnel onto pitch | Entrance and payoff | Realistic gait and contact shadows |
| `S010` | 16.0 | 2.0 | Child in approved jersey runs toward family | Human impact | Dignified, natural behavior |
| `S011` | 18.0 | 2.0 | Wide hero hold in stadium | Title and disclosure space | Stable hold; clean safe areas |

The fan/city impact montage can be a separate film or cutdown. Do not overload the reveal master with every store, street, phone, child, café, and crowd idea.

## 5. Example profile-reveal prompt

```text
SHOT S008 — CONTROLLED PROFILE REVEAL
DURATION: 2.0 seconds | FORMAT: 16:9 | PURPOSE: first recognizable likeness reveal

REFERENCE CONTROL
Use CHAR-01 only within the approved likeness scope. Preserve facial structure, hairstyle, age appearance, skin tone, and proportions. Use WARD-01 for the jersey cut, stripe layout, collar, fabric, and palette. Use the approved jersey plate for exact marks.

OPENING STATE
The player stands at the tunnel edge with his back three-quarters to camera. The crowd is soft and distant. His face is not visible.

PRIMARY ACTION
He turns his head slightly toward the pitch and settles into a calm side profile.

CAMERA
Slow controlled arc of approximately 25 degrees, shoulder-height medium close-up, no zoom, natural 65 mm lens character.

ENVIRONMENT AND LIGHT
Stadium floodlights create a restrained rim along the shoulder and hair. Keep believable tunnel shadows and subtle haze.

ENDING STATE
Hold the side profile for at least 18 clean frames before the cut.

CONTINUITY HANDOFF
Preserve left-to-right screen direction for the entrance in S009.

NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No identity drift, face morphing, jersey redesign, invented marks, exaggerated emotion, generic press-conference staging, crowd duplication, or focus pumping.
```

## 6. Cultural and editorial controls

- Use verified Trabzon references for architecture, weather, streets, stores, scarves, flags, signage, and matchday behavior.
- Include a plausible range of ages and appearances without reducing nationality to facial stereotypes.
- Keep children and fans as people with natural reactions, not props for celebrity worship.
- Do not fabricate headlines, quotes, contracts, press conferences, or official social posts.
- Put exact `10`, `SALAH`, club marks, title, and disclosure into approved plates or post-production.

## 7. Case-study evidence

A credible case study should show:

1. source references and rights status;
2. shot manifest and prompt revision history;
3. continuity contact sheets across the entire reveal;
4. failed-shot diagnosis and repair examples;
5. final edit specifications and disclosure placement;
6. what was generated, composited, licensed, or filmed.

A few attractive frames prove taste, not continuity or production reliability.
