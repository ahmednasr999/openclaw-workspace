# Video analysis rubric

Read this reference for detailed creative analysis, competitor research, or content-system recommendations. Use judgment; not every video needs every dimension.

## Analysis dimensions

1. **Hook and promise**
   - What happens in the first three seconds?
   - Is the viewer given a clear tension, benefit, novelty, or open loop?
   - Does the opening earn attention without misleading the audience?

2. **Message architecture**
   - Identify setup, tension, mechanism, proof, resolution, and CTA.
   - Note where information is repeated, delayed, or unsupported.
   - Distinguish the creator's stated claim from the analyst's interpretation.

3. **Proof and credibility**
   - Look for demonstrations, examples, data, source references, lived experience, and visible product evidence.
   - Penalize confident claims that depend only on assertion or borrowed authority.

4. **Visual grammar**
   - Assess framing, composition, lighting, color, typography, B-roll, screen recordings, overlays, and continuity.
   - Explain what each device contributes. Do not treat cinematic polish as strategy by itself.

5. **Editing and audio**
   - Assess shot duration, cuts, pattern interrupts, zooms, captions, music, silence, sound effects, and speech clarity.
   - Connect editing choices to comprehension, emphasis, energy, or retention; label that causal link as interpretation.

6. **Platform fit**
   - Consider aspect ratio, safe zones, mobile legibility, silent viewing, pacing, duration, loop potential, and CTA behavior.
   - Do not assume one platform's conventions transfer unchanged to another.

7. **Audience and strategic fit**
   - Identify the apparent audience, awareness level, pain point, and desired next action.
   - For executive content, prefer credibility, specificity, and operating insight over trend mimicry.

8. **Adaptation potential**
   - Extract the mechanism, not the surface treatment.
   - Produce original angles grounded in the user's evidence, voice, audience, and goals.
   - State what should not be copied and why.

## Evidence quality

- **High confidence:** directly audible/visible, clear caption, or verified metadata.
- **Medium confidence:** strong inference supported by multiple cues.
- **Low confidence:** plausible but weakly evidenced; include only when useful and label it.

Prefer a short table of strong evidence over a long list of speculative observations.
