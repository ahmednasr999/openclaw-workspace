# 3. Decision authority and approval hierarchy

## Authority levels

| Level | Action class | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | Read-only inspection, retrieval, calculation | Automatic |
| A1 | Reversible local artifacts and workspace state | Automatic with audit |
| A2 | Exact pre-approved workflow action | Automatic if payload, target and time window match |
| A3 | New external communication, public action or production change | Explicit Ahmed approval |
| A4 | Credentials, money, legal commitments, destructive actions, public exposure | Explicit action-bound confirmation and maintenance controls |
| Emergency | Active security or integrity threat | Contain automatically, notify Ahmed, no destructive recovery without approval |

Approval must bind to:

- Exact action and payload.
- Target system and recipient.
- Workflow run.
- Expiration time.
- Maximum repetitions.
- Permitted rollback.
- Approver identity.

Broad conversational approval cannot authorize unrelated future actions.

## Separation of duties

A consequential workflow must separate:

1. Producer.
2. Policy checker.
3. Approver, where required.
4. Executor.
5. Verifier.

No agent may both create, approve, execute and verify the same high-impact action.

## Workflow contracts

Every production workflow must declare:

- Owner roles.
- Purpose and business outcome.
- Trigger and inputs.
- Typed outputs.
- Approval class.
- Data classification.
- SLO and timeout.
- Idempotency key.
- Retry limit.
- Verification method.
- Recovery and rollback.
- Observability fields.

An incomplete contract cannot enter production.

## Policy checks

Machine-readable policy checks must reject:

- Missing ownership.
- Unsupported approval.
- Unclassified data.
- Unbounded retries.
- Missing timeout or idempotency.
- Unpinned plugins or models.
- External actions without verification.
- Workflows without recovery and SLO definitions.

## Approval rules

Approvals must bind to the exact:

- Action.
- Payload hash.
- Target and recipient.
- Workflow run.
- Validity period.
- Repetition limit.
- Rollback authority.
- Approver identity.

Expired, ambiguous, or mismatched approval fails closed.

## Tool permissions

- Agents receive capabilities, not unrestricted environments.
- Permissions are least-privilege and workflow-scoped.
- Sensitive operations require purpose-bound credentials.
- Tool results are typed and auditable.
- Denials and privilege requests are recorded.

## Prompt validation

Production prompts must pass:

- Role and authority validation.
- Unsupported-assumption checks.
- Evidence and citation requirements.
- Output-schema validation.
- Injection-resistance checks.
- Token-budget checks.
- Approval and stop-condition checks.
- Regression tests against known failure cases.

## Architecture reviews

Required for:

- New agents or executive offices.
- New workflow engines or schedulers.
- New D2-D3 data flows.
- New external-write capability.
- New plugins with network, filesystem, credential, or execution access.
- SLO or authority changes.
- Removal of recovery controls.

Reviews assess ownership, coupling, security, recovery, cost, observability, and constitutional compliance.

## Production readiness gates

A production capability requires:

1. Named owners.
2. Versioned contract.
3. Proven output quality on real samples.
4. Security and data review.
5. Approval classification.
6. Failure and retry tests.
7. Duplicate-prevention proof.
8. Recovery and rollback proof.
9. Telemetry and dashboard integration.
10. Independent control sign-off.

## Audit logs

The audit ledger must be:

- Append-only.
- Timestamped and identity-bound.
- Correlated across trigger, decision, approval, execution, and verification.
- Redacted of secrets.
- Retained according to data classification.
- Independently queryable from the affected workflow.

## SLO reporting

- Continuous calculation for critical controls.
- Weekly owner review.
- Monthly error-budget review.
- Quarterly constitutional review.
- Exhausted budgets freeze nonessential changes.
- Zero-budget breaches create immediate incidents.

## Exception management

Every exception must record:

- Exception ID and requested owner.
- Exact constitutional clause affected.
- Business justification.
- Scope and affected workflows.
- Start and expiration.
- Compensating controls.
- Residual risk.
- Monitoring plan.
- Rollback or termination condition.
- Approving authorities.

Approval rules:

- Low-risk A0-A1 exceptions require business, technical, and control owner approval.
- A2-A4, D2-D3, security, public-action, or zero-error-budget exceptions require Ahmed.
- Emergency exceptions last no more than 24 hours.
- Standard exceptions last no more than 30 days.
- Exceptions never renew automatically.
- Expired exceptions fail closed.
- Open exceptions are reviewed weekly.

Non-waivable rules:

- No fabricated critical facts.
- No hidden external actions.
- No deliberate credential exposure.
- No silent authority expansion.
- No suppression of material incidents.
- No execution outside legal or ethical boundaries.
